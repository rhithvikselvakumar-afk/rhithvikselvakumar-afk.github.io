Nitrogen Laser — Pulse Amplifier Group

Team of three / Pulse Amplifier

Building one part of a larger system.

In my Cornell ENGRI lab, I worked with two teammates to build the pulse amplifier for a nitrogen laser. Our subsystem was intended to connect with the work of the pulse-generator and chassis teams.

My contribution

I helped build the pulse amplifier as part of a three-person team. This gave me hands-on experience soldering electrical components and understanding how individual circuit elements work together within a larger assembly.

Understanding the circuit

The course manual describes the amplifier as the interface between the pulse generator and the laser chassis. It uses a silicon-controlled rectifier (SCR), capacitors, and a pulse transformer to produce the trigger pulse for the chassis discharge circuit.

Working on this subsystem helped me understand transformer operation and why capacitor placement and circuit layout matter for controlling electrical noise and reducing unintended SCR triggering.

Circuit background: Fall 2026 AEP 1100 Complete Manual, “Pulse Amplifier,” printed pages 9–11. The circuit design was supplied by the course; my team’s work focused on building the subsystem.

Testing & a setback

During our first test, a short circuit damaged the pulse generator. This was a setback in testing the overall system. It reinforced for me that assembly, inspection, and testing are important parts of engineering work.

What I learned

  • Soldering components and making electrical connections.
  • Understanding the role of transformers in a pulse circuit.
  • Considering capacitor placement and noise when working with an SCR.
  • Coordinating a subsystem with other teams’ work.
  • Recognizing the importance of lab safety around lasers and electrical equipment.
