Background · Interests · Looking ahead

About me.

I’m Rhithvik Selvakumar, a Cornell student interested in the circuits, components, and design decisions that make technology work.

I’m in Cornell University’s Class of 2030, intending to major in Electrical and Computer Engineering. I enjoy understanding how a system works, then using that understanding to design, build, or improve something.

My engineering background began at Round Rock High School, where courses such as PLTW Digital Electronics and Engineering Design and Development let me connect classroom concepts with practical projects. Working with Multisim and breadboards helped me move between circuit design, simulation, and physical construction. On our electric-bike conversion project, I took responsibility for research, calculations, electrical diagrams, and cost optimization, while also helping with wiring and CAD.

Learning through projects

NASA HAS and Moonshot gave me the opportunity to explore aerospace design with a team. I contributed reduced-gravity research and CAD work to a spacesuit proposal, thinking about how cooling, glove function, and wearable components fit into a larger system. Through Future City, I led 3D modeling for a sustainable ocean-city concept and explored how energy, infrastructure, and community needs connect.

At Cornell, I’ve continued building through the nitrogen laser lab, working with a team of three on the pulse amplifier. Soldering, circuit layout, and subsystem integration have given me more experience with the details involved in making a shared design work.

Sharing what I learn

Teaching is another important part of my experience. Through CAD mentoring and STEM tutoring at Leap4ward, I’ve helped students work through technical ideas and develop their own designs. Explaining a process to someone else encourages me to understand it more clearly myself, and leading shared projects has helped me practice communication, planning, and teamwork.

What’s next

I hope to join a Cornell project team and get involved in research around chip design and fabrication. I want to build on my experience with digital circuits and learn more about how the components inside electronic systems are designed and made.

Outside engineering, swimming has been an important part of my life. I also enjoy the opportunity to teach, collaborate, and keep learning from the people around me.
