My role

Turning shared ideas into a city model

Design and modeling

I led the 3D modeling component of our sustainable ocean-city project, helping translate our team’s ideas into a coordinated digital model. My work focused on the city’s structural plan, power grids, and renewable energy storage.

The challenge was to think beyond individual buildings and consider how the city’s systems would support one another in a marine environment.

Leadership and mentoring

As a co-founder and 3D modeling mentor for the Future City club at Leap4ward, I taught modeling skills and helped students explore sustainable engineering. I supported recruitment, community presentations, and the program’s expansion into middle school teams.

This gave me experience explaining technical ideas, organizing shared work, and helping other students develop their own designs.

Ocean-city project

A floating city with a circular approach

Our earlier concept explored a floating city that would use recycled ocean plastic as a building material. We considered renewable energy, energy storage, resource management, and the structural challenges of a marine setting.

People’s Choice & Best EssayTeam recognition at the Future City international competition for our ocean-city project.

For me, the digital model became a way to communicate the city as a connected system: where people would live, how infrastructure would fit together, and how energy could be supplied and stored.

Pivô City · A separate team concept

Turning coastal challenges into design opportunities

Pivô City imagines a future transformation of Recife, Brazil. Our team’s proposal connects flood resilience with food production, cold-chain transport, and waste recovery. Its name draws on the Portuguese idea of pivoting: redirecting a challenge into an opportunity.

A speculative future-city proposal. The systems below describe the team’s design vision and would require further engineering and validation.

01 / GROW

Food production

Vertical farms, hydroponics, rooftop gardens, and agrivoltaic housing connect food production to the spaces where residents live.

02 / MOVE

AquaPod logistics

The proposed AquaPods use waterways to move food. The concept combines insulated storage, phase-change materials, ripening sensors, and coordinated delivery planning.

03 / RECOVER

Waste and energy

The proposal links food-waste collection to bio-electrochemical energy concepts, alongside a diversified energy network and local backup storage.

Design trade-offs

The essay considers how protected mangroves can limit shoreline development, how complex digital coordination creates dependencies, and how energy infrastructure competes for space. These trade-offs make redundancy, zoning, and careful integration central to the proposal.

Team collaboration

The Pivô City essay was developed by Vineeth Erram, Vishaal Erram, Arya Salvaji, and Rhithvik Selvakumar at Leap4ward Academy, with educator Ramya Rajendran.

Our shared proposal brings together ideas from civil, environmental, agricultural, marine, and computer engineering.

Reflection

What I learned

Systems thinking

I learned to look at how energy, transportation, housing, and resource use interact. A decision that improves one part of a city can create a new constraint elsewhere.

CAD as communication

Modeling gave me a way to make ideas visible, compare spatial arrangements, and explain how different parts of a proposal fit together.

Research and trade-offs

I gained practice connecting research to a design choice and discussing its benefits, limitations, and assumptions.

Teamwork

I practiced combining different ideas into one design, coordinating responsibilities, and keeping the shared goal in view through a long project.

Teaching and leadership

Mentoring other students taught me to break modeling tasks into manageable steps and adapt explanations to different experience levels.

Presenting a vision

Essays, models, and community presentations helped me explain both the technical choices and the reasons a design could matter to the people living in it.
