Engineering for a different environment.

Through NASA High School Aerospace Scholars (HAS), I explored mission planning, spacecraft modeling, and the challenges of living and working beyond Earth. I developed CAD concepts, completed hands-on design activities, and documented results in an engineering notebook.

During Moonshot, I worked with Team Foxtrot 02 on a spacesuit proposal for lunar south-pole exploration. My focus connected reduced-gravity research with temperature regulation, glove functionality, and the design of suit components.

Team Foxtrot 02 · Moonshot

Supporting astronauts on the lunar surface

Our team explored how a spacesuit could support extravehicular activity at the lunar south pole. The mission brief asked us to consider a crew of six to ten, a six-month to one-year surface stay, and a combination of testing environments.

My role

I served as the team’s gravity issues technician. I researched balance, movement, and heat-transfer considerations in reduced gravity and helped connect that research to the suit proposal.

My contributions included cooling-system concepts, glove-function research, and CAD work on gloves, a helmet, and an arm-mounted heads-up display (HUD).

Working across the team

I worked with teammates to connect the gravity research to glove materials and temperature regulation. Our shared research document and presentation scripts helped bring the different subtopics into one mission proposal.

The final presentation represents our team’s combined research and design work.

01 / RESEARCH

Reduced gravity

I explored how changes in gravity affect movement, balance, and buoyancy-driven airflow, then considered what those differences meant for a suit design.

02 / SYSTEMS

Thermal management

I helped develop a cooling concept using active circulation, connecting the heat-transfer research to the team’s broader life-support proposal.

03 / CAD

Human-centered components

I modeled suit components and considered how gloves, a helmet, and a wearable display could support an astronaut’s tasks.

How the team developed the glove concept

Our glove design script connects the traction research Olivia and I contributed to the team’s CAD and prototype iterations. It documents how teammates explored articulation, fingertip geometry, material choices, and sensor integration.

From CAD to a physical mock-up

The first CAD iteration used flexible finger sections, a cuff ring, and fingertip caps. A later iteration added origami-style joints informed by Max’s research. Physical mock-ups explored different fingertip treatments, wrist fit, and outer materials; the revised version used cardboard fingertip caps and hot-glue dots as a simple stand-in for a textured grip surface.

Electronics and a testing plan

The team script describes an Arduino dust-alert mock-up using a sensor, LCD, LEDs, and a buzzer to communicate changing readings. It also outlines a planned three-person penny-pickup test to assess comfort, mobility, and fine-object handling.

These were classroom design demonstrations. The script provides a proposed user-test procedure, but does not report its results or establish performance under lunar conditions.

HAS coursework · Individual explorations

Learning through building and testing

My earlier HAS work combined research and digital design with small physical prototypes. These activities gave me practice documenting an idea, testing it, and using the results to decide what to change.

Crew module drop tests

My log records nine trials at heights from 1 to 3 meters. After the first module drifted left, I adjusted the string lengths. When the parachute tore during a 3-meter drop, I changed its attachment location and repeated the test; the final entry records no issues.

Heavy-lift balloon rocket

I compared predicted payloads with recorded results. After an initial trial lifted no payload, later entries recorded 16, 40, 50, and 60 grams. I also documented adding a paper insert to keep the clips in place.

Multipurpose tool

My tool concept combined measuring, a camera, lighting, and small storage compartments in a handheld form. I used a simplified volume estimate to consider the material remaining after hollowing out the grip and compartments.

The estimate reduced the outer volume of 43.2 cm³ by 18.36 cm³ of hollow sections, leaving 24.84 cm³. This was an initial geometry calculation, rather than a finished mass or manufacturing analysis.

Reflection

What I learned

Research into design

I learned to connect research to a specific design question, such as how thermal behavior or mobility could influence a suit component.

Systems thinking

I gained practice considering how one choice affects other parts of a design, including cooling, power, weight, usability, and the mission environment.

Iteration and evidence

Prototype tests taught me to record failures, change a specific feature, and test again. Keeping a log made the reasons for each revision easier to explain.

CAD and constraints

I practiced using Tinkercad and Fusion 360 to communicate concepts, while considering space, geometry, and how a person would interact with a design.

Teamwork and documentation

Moonshot gave me practice contributing to a shared research document, coordinating related design decisions, and keeping my work understandable to teammates.

Technical communication

I developed my ability to turn research and design reasoning into scripts, visuals, and presentations that fit within a larger team story.

Evidence shown here comes from my HAS notebook, prototype photographs, design notes, presentation recordings, and our Foxtrot 02 research, subtopic scripts, and glove design script.
